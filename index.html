Method

The same procedure is applied to every variable and location.

StepProcedureReason
1. DataMonthly values for 1981–2025 at the selected coordinates, from the NASA POWER service. Meteorological variables are from the MERRA-2 reanalysis. Solar radiation and cloud variables are from CERES satellite products (from 1984). Aerosol and UV variables begin in 2001.A record of several decades is needed to separate a trend from year-to-year variability.
2. Seasonal cycleThe long-term mean for each calendar month is subtracted from every value for that month, giving monthly anomalies.The seasonal cycle is usually much larger than the trend and would obscure it.
3. Annual valuesOne value is computed for each complete calendar year (a mean, or a total for counts such as frost days).Annual values are less serially correlated than monthly values, as the tests assume.
4. Rate of changeSen’s slope (the median of the slopes between all pairs of years) and an ordinary least-squares fit with a 95% confidence interval.Sen’s slope is robust to outliers. The two estimates serve as a check on each other.
5. SignificanceMann-Kendall test and a two-sided t-test on the least-squares slope. A trend is reported as significant only if both give p < 0.05.p < 0.05 means that a trend at least this strong would be expected less than 5% of the time if no real trend existed.
6. DiagnosticsLag-1 autocorrelation of the residuals is calculated, and a note is shown when it exceeds 0.4.Serial correlation reduces the effective sample size and makes p-values too small.

Limitations

The data are gridded model and satellite products with a resolution of roughly 50 km or coarser, not station measurements. Only linear trends are tested. A significant trend shows that a change occurred; it does not identify the cause. When many grid points are tested, about 1 in 20 is expected to appear significant by chance, so isolated significant points should be interpreted with caution. Precipitation and soil-moisture trends from a reanalysis are less certain than temperature trends and should be confirmed with satellite precipitation data (GPM) using the Import data tab.

Verification

The statistical routines were checked against SciPy (linregress, kendalltau and theilslopes) on a test series.
